Why Is My PNG Not Transparent? Every Cause and Fix

If your PNG isn't showing as transparent, one of three things is true: the file never had transparency in the first place, the transparency was flattened when the file was saved, or the file is transparent and the app you're viewing it in is simply drawing it on a white background. The last one is by far the most common, and it isn't a problem with your file at all.

This page walks through how to tell which case you're in, then gives the fix for each app where this comes up.

Step 1: Check whether the PNG is actually transparent

Before changing anything, find out whether the file has real transparency. Do this first — it decides everything that follows.

The checkerboard test. Open the PNG in any image editor (Preview on Mac, Paint 3D or Photos on Windows, GIMP, Photopea). If the area around your subject shows a grey-and-white checkerboard pattern, the transparency is real. If it shows solid white, there is no transparency in the file.

The dark background test. If you don't want to open an editor, drag the PNG onto a dark-coloured slide, a dark browser tab, or any dark surface. Real transparency lets the dark colour through. A white box means the white is baked in.

Check the real file type. A file named logo.png is not necessarily a PNG. Some apps rename on export. On Windows, right-click → Properties. On Mac, select the file and press Cmd+I. If it reports JPEG, that's your answer: JPG cannot store transparency at all, so a white background is unavoidable until you re-export as PNG.

Case A: The file genuinely has no transparency

If the checkerboard never appeared, the background is part of the image. No setting in Word, Canva or anywhere else will remove it — the pixels are white, and they have to be cut out.

The two usual reasons:

  • It was saved as JPG. JPG has no alpha channel. Every transparent pixel becomes white (or sometimes black) on save.
  • It was flattened on export. Many editors have a "flatten image" or "merge visible layers" step, and some export dialogs default to removing the alpha channel. The layers looked transparent on screen; the exported file isn't.

The fix is to remove the background properly and export as PNG. You can do that here in a few seconds — the cutout runs on your image and gives you back a file with a real alpha channel.

When you export, two things matter: choose PNG (not JPG, not "flatten"), and don't re-save the result through an app that strips alpha. If you need the file for print, keep it in RGB — converting a PNG to CMYK usually discards transparency and fills it with white.

Case B: The file is transparent, but the app shows white

This is the case most people are actually in, and it surprises them. Transparency is not a colour. It's the absence of one. So a transparent PNG takes on whatever sits behind it — and in a document, a slide or a spreadsheet, what sits behind it is a white page.

Your PNG is fine. What you're seeing is the page showing through.

That means the fix is never "fix the PNG" — it's either to put the colour you want behind the image, or to stop the app from adding a white matte of its own. The details differ per app, so here they are one by one.

Microsoft Word

Word draws images on the document's white page fill, so a transparent PNG looks white even when it isn't. Insert through Insert → Pictures → This Device rather than copy-paste; pasting routes the image through the clipboard, which can flatten the alpha channel. If you need a colour behind the logo, set a shape or text-box fill underneath it instead of expecting the PNG to supply it. Full walkthrough for Word.

Microsoft Excel

Same cause as Word: the worksheet's cell fill is white, and the image sits on top of it. The thing that catches people out in Excel is that a picture placed over cells with a white fill will look boxed even on a coloured sheet. Change the fill of the cells underneath, not the image. Full walkthrough for Excel.

PowerPoint and Keynote

Both render transparent PNGs correctly, but both sit the image on a white slide background by default. Put the image over a coloured slide, or set the slide background, and the transparency becomes visible. In PowerPoint, avoid the "Set Transparent Color" tool for this — it keys out a single colour and leaves hard edges, which is a different thing from alpha transparency. Detailed guides for PowerPoint and Keynote.

Google Docs and Google Slides

Google Docs re-encodes uploaded images, and in some upload paths it adds a white matte. Uploading the file directly (Insert → Image → Upload from computer) preserves transparency more reliably than pasting from another tab or inserting by URL. Full walkthrough for Google Docs.

Outlook email signatures

This one is the exception where the app really is at fault. Outlook adds a white background to signature images, and it's most visible when the recipient is reading in Dark Mode: your logo appears as a white rectangle on a dark message. There's no setting to turn it off, so the usual answer is a logo designed to sit on white, or a version of the signature that doesn't depend on transparency. Full walkthrough for Outlook signatures.

Cricut Design Space

Design Space shows a white background on uploaded PNGs in some views even when the alpha channel is intact, which matters for Print Then Cut. The upload flow's own "remove background" step and the cut-line preview are the two places to check before assuming the file is wrong. Full walkthrough for Cricut Design Space.

Printing

Printers have no transparent ink. Any see-through area falls back to the colour of the paper, which is almost always white. If you're printing onto plain white stock with nothing behind the image, a white result is expected and correct — add the background you actually want inside your document. More on why transparent PNGs print white.

Canva and Figma

Both handle alpha correctly. In Canva, the thing to watch is the export step: downloading as JPG, or as PNG without ticking Transparent background, bakes the background in. In Figma, make sure no frame or background rectangle sits behind your export selection, and that the export isn't set to JPG.

Web browsers

If a transparent PNG looks white on a web page, the element behind it almost always has a white background colour set in CSS. The image is doing its job. Inspect the parent element rather than re-exporting the file.

Case C: The background shows black instead of white

Black is the same problem wearing different clothes. Some apps and some export paths fill discarded alpha with black rather than white — Premiere Pro, a few PDF viewers, Photoshop's "Save As" when the format doesn't support alpha, and certain CMS thumbnail generators are common sources.

The diagnosis is identical: run the checkerboard test. If the checkerboard is there, the file is fine and the app is compositing it onto black. If it isn't, the alpha channel was dropped on save and you need to re-export. More on PNGs that show a black background.

Case D: Transparent, but with a white outline

Sometimes transparency works and there's still a thin white fringe or halo around the subject. That isn't a transparency failure — it's leftover edge pixels from the original background, which were partly white and got kept at the boundary.

This shows up most on hair, fur, glass and anything with a soft edge, and it's most visible once you place the cutout on a dark background. The fix is better edge handling at the cutout stage rather than anything you do afterwards in Word or Canva. How to fix white edges after background removal.

How to get a properly transparent PNG

  1. Upload your image to the background remover.
  2. Let the cutout run, then look for the checkerboard in the preview — that's your confirmation the alpha channel is real.
  3. Download as PNG. Not JPG, not WebP if the destination doesn't support alpha.
  4. Place it over the background you actually want, in whatever app you're using.
  5. Don't re-save it through an app that flattens — that undoes the whole thing.

Frequently Asked Questions

How do I know for certain whether my PNG is transparent?

Open it in an image editor or drop it onto a dark background. A grey-and-white checkerboard means the transparency is real. Solid white or solid black means the alpha channel isn't there.

Why does my PNG look transparent in Preview but white in Word?

Because Preview shows transparency as a checkerboard, and Word shows it as the white page underneath. Both are displaying the same file correctly — only the thing behind it differs.

Can a JPG have a transparent background?

No. The JPG format has no alpha channel. If you need transparency, the file has to be a PNG (or WebP, or another format that supports alpha).

Why does transparency disappear when I save in Photoshop?

Usually because the chosen format doesn't support alpha, or the layers were flattened onto a white background before export. Export as PNG with the background layer hidden.

Does converting a PNG to PDF keep transparency?

Sometimes. An RGB-to-CMYK conversion normally drops the alpha channel and fills it with white. Keep the export in RGB, or place the PNG inside your layout app rather than converting the file itself.
